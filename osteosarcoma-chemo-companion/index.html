🚨 When to call your cancer team now

During chemotherapy, infections and some side effects can become serious quickly. Follow your cancer team’s urgent-contact instructions. Seek urgent advice for:

  • 🌡️ A temperature of 38°C or higher
  • 🥶 Chills or shaking
  • 😮‍💨 Trouble breathing, a new cough or worsening shortness of breath
  • 🧠 New confusion, severe dizziness or fainting
  • 🩸 Unusual or uncontrolled bleeding or bruising
  • 💧 Being unable to eat or drink enough, repeated vomiting, or signs of dehydration
  • 🦷 Mouth or throat pain that makes it difficult to eat, drink or sleep
  • 🔴 Redness, swelling, pain or discharge around a catheter, port or wound

Emergency: If symptoms are severe or life-threatening, call your local emergency number. In Australia, call 000.

🌿 What may help today

💧 Fluids

Take regular small sips if drinking a full glass feels difficult. Follow any fluid targets or restrictions given by the oncology team.

🍽️ Food

Small, frequent meals can be easier than large meals. Choose foods that are tolerable today, and ask the cancer team or oncology dietitian if eating becomes difficult.

😴 Energy

Fatigue is common during chemotherapy. Rest when needed and tell the treatment team if fatigue is severe, new or worsening.

🦷 Mouth care

Tell the cancer team early about sores, ulcers, bleeding gums, dry mouth, pain or difficulty swallowing. Ask them what mouthwash or dental care is safe during treatment.

🤢 Nausea

Use anti-nausea medicines exactly as prescribed. Tell the cancer team if nausea or vomiting is not controlled.

🚶 Movement

Bone tumours can affect fracture risk and mobility. Follow the oncology, orthopaedic and physiotherapy team’s advice before changing activity.

🗣️ Questions for the next appointment

About symptoms

  • Which symptoms should I call about immediately?
  • What temperature threshold should I use?
  • Which number should I use during the day and after hours?
  • What can we do if nausea, pain, bowel changes or mouth sores are not controlled?
  • Should I report new thinking or memory changes?

About this chemo cycle

  • Which medicines and doses am I receiving this cycle?
  • Can you write down my chemotherapy medicine names and doses for emergency care?
  • When are my blood counts expected to be lowest?
  • Are there foods, supplements or medicines I should avoid?
  • Should I report tingling, numbness, ringing in my ears or hearing changes?
  • What activity is safe for my affected bone?

🤝 Practical and emotional support

Nurses, pharmacists, physiotherapists, social workers, psychologists and oncology dietitians can all be part of cancer care.

In Australia, Cancer Council 13 11 20 offers confidential cancer information and practical or emotional support.
