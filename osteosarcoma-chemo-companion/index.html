Urgent help

When to call your cancer team now

During chemotherapy, infections and some side effects can become serious quickly. Follow your cancer team’s urgent-contact instructions.

🌡️ Temperature 38°C or higher🥶 Chills or shaking😮‍💨 Trouble breathing or a new/worse cough🧠 New confusion, severe dizziness or fainting🩸 Unusual or uncontrolled bleeding or bruising💧 Repeated vomiting or signs of dehydration🦷 Mouth or throat pain stopping eating, drinking or sleep🔴 Redness, swelling, pain or discharge around a port, catheter or wound
☎️ Cancer-team contact: Add your oncology urgent-contact details below so they are easy to find.

Emergency: If symptoms are severe or life-threatening, call your local emergency number. In Australia, call 000.

For today

Small things that may make today easier

Choose what is useful. You do not need to do everything.

💧

Fluids

Regular small sips can be easier than a full glass. Follow any fluid targets or restrictions from your oncology team.

🍽️

Food

Small, frequent meals may be easier. Ask your cancer team or oncology dietitian for help if eating is difficult.

😴

Energy

Fatigue is common. Rest when needed and tell your team if it becomes severe, new or noticeably worse.

🤢

Nausea

Use anti-nausea medicines exactly as prescribed. Tell the cancer team if nausea or vomiting is not controlled.

🦷

Mouth care

Report sores, ulcers, bleeding gums, dry mouth, pain or swallowing problems early.

🚶

Movement

Bone tumours can affect fracture risk. Follow oncology, orthopaedic and physiotherapy advice before changing activity.

Questions you might want to ask

About symptoms

  • Which symptoms should I call about immediately?
  • What temperature threshold should I use?
  • Which number should I use during the day and after hours?
  • What can we do if nausea, pain, bowel changes or mouth sores are not controlled?
  • Should I report new thinking or memory changes?

About this chemo cycle

  • Which medicines and doses am I receiving this cycle?
  • Can you write down my chemotherapy medicine names and doses for emergency care?
  • When are my blood counts expected to be lowest?
  • Are there foods, supplements or medicines I should avoid?
  • Should I report tingling, numbness, ringing in my ears or hearing changes?
  • What activity is safe for my affected bone?

People can help

🤝 Practical and emotional support

Nurses, pharmacists, physiotherapists, social workers, psychologists and oncology dietitians can all be part of cancer care.

In Australia, Cancer Council 13 11 20 offers confidential cancer information and practical or emotional support.
